## English version (if the jury needs it, about the same length)

> I have only one photo of myself as a child, and that's true for many African families: we have so few photos that we forget our ancestors. *Jua* is a short film: a family in Kinshasa is moving house, finds an old photo with no name, restores it, and gives Mama Nzeba her place back on the wall.
>
> I produced everything with agents:
> - **Claude Code** was my director and editor. It wrote the prompts, picked the takes, and cut the film frame by frame.
> - In **Arcads**, **Nano Banana 2** restores the photo, and **Seedance 2.5** creates the scenes, because it keeps characters consistent best.
> - **ElevenLabs** gives the mother a real African accent and emotion.
>
> The hardest part was consistency. AI redraws faces in every shot, so we locked character sheets and started each shot from the previous shot's last frame. Then there was the handwritten name, gaps in the sound, and cuts matched pixel by pixel.
>
> What I learned: AI can restore an image, but only people can give back a name. And sound tells half the story; Tabu Ley's *Pitié* never stops.
>
> What I'd do better: record a real family voice instead of an AI one, have the Lingala checked by a native speaker, and lock the story earlier.
>
> *Jua*: we restore the image, and we remember the person together.

## Recording tips
- Film in landscape, face to camera, with window light on your face. Aim for 1:25, so there is margin before 1:30.
- Speak one block per breath, and pause for about 1 s between blocks so the cutaways can go in.
- Mention "Claude Code", "Arcads", "Seedance 2.5", "Nano Banana 2" and "ElevenLabs" clearly: they answer the "tools" question.
