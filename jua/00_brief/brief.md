## Honesty rules (non-negotiable)
- The original is shown first, long enough to see what changed, and **last**, side by side with the restoration. The AI version never replaces it.
- Motion is limited to light, fabric, or a camera move. **No speaking, blinking-on-cue, smiling, or invented action** on a real face.
- No AI-generated detail (clothing color, background, place) is presented as fact. If colorized, say so on the end card.
- Name, place, and memory come only from the witness, in their own voice.

## Fictional proof-of-concept mode
- Portrait generated from scratch (no real archival face), testimony voiced by a consenting person reading a written script.
- End card must say: **"Fictional proof of concept. No real person is depicted."**
- Keep a concrete place/year anyway — specificity is what moves people. [FICTIONAL PLACE, YEAR]

## Limbic narration (the track's challenge)
Tell the story to the emotional brain (senses, memory, rhythm), not through an explaining voice.
- **No narrator, no voice-over.** The only words are the witness's, spoken *inside* the scene: room acoustics, breath, hesitations, a laugh. It should sound like a memory, not a script.
- **The one true detail should be sensory:** a smell, a song she hummed, the fabric she wore, the sound of her kitchen. Sensory memories hit harder than facts.
- **Sound carries the plot:** paper → an unresolved motif → a silence held on a breath → the motif resolves under the pen.
- **Touch is the proof of feeling:** a thumb on the print's edge, a pen hesitating before the name.
- **If the witness remembers a song or hums a tune**, record it: cue B can grow out of that hum. The film's music then literally comes from her memory.

## Muted test
Muted, a stranger should still see: worn photo → face becomes clear → someone writes her name on the sleeve. The subtitles carry the testimony. Target: **YES**.
