Purpose: give the fictional portrait and the present-day room only details that trace to a source. Nothing here describes a real person. Style references (Depara, Studio 3Z) are **style only** — no real photograph or real sitter is copied.

## 5 facts

1. **1972 is the first year of "authenticité".** Mobutu announced the campaign on 2 January 1972, to replace colonial-era names, customs and dress with "Africanized" ones. — [1972 in Zaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972_in_Zaire), [Zaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaire), [The Hedgehog Review: Authenticity in Fashion](https://hedgehogreview.com/issues/authenticity/articles/authenticity-in-fashion). *Confidence: high (several consistent sources).*

2. **Women's dress: the wrapped liputa in wax print.** Under authenticité "women wore the wrapped liputa"; women "wrapped themselves from waist to ankle with African wax print cloth, ironically made in Holland". — [Abacost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abacost), [The Hedgehog Review](https://hedgehogreview.com/issues/authenticity/articles/authenticity-in-fashion). *Confidence: medium-high. **Timing caveat:** the ordinance prohibiting wigs, trousers and skirts for women came after Mobutu's January 1973 trip to China — so in 1972 the liputa was the direction of travel, not yet law.*

3. **Hair: wigs and straightening were targeted; plaits, braids and headwraps stayed.** Zaire "banned hair straightening and wigs"; "plaited styles, braids and headwraps continued to be worn by women from all classes". — [Hair as Freedom (BUALA)](https://www.buala.org/en/to-read/hair-as-freedom), [Lolwe: Hair as Freedom](https://lolwe.org/hair-as-freedom/), [Face2Face Africa](https://face2faceafrica.com/article/congolese-natural-hair). *Confidence: medium. The exact braid style in 1972 is not established.*

4. **The headwrap is often cut from the same cloth as the liputa.** A headwrap "styled from the same fabric as the liputa"; the full set is cut from one two-by-six-yard pagne so pattern and colour match. — [Wrapper (clothing)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrapper_(clothing)), [Head tie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_tie), [OMIREN Styles](https://omirenstyles.com/kongo-women-styles/). *Confidence: **low-medium** — these describe current practice; not confirmed specifically for 1972.*

5. **Kinshasa studio portraiture in 1972: studios with changing painted backdrops, black-and-white prints.** Studio 3Z (Ambroise Ngaimoko) opened in Kitambo, Kinshasa, in 1971; clients remember "the constantly changing backdrops"; its 1970s portraits record the youth of Kinshasa, Sape and body-builders, with poses influenced by record covers and magazines. Jean Depara closed his studio in 1966 and photographed the city's nightlife. — [Ambroise Ngaimoko (Studio 3Z), CAAC Pigozzi](http://www.caacart.com/pigozzi-artist.php?m=84), [MoMA post: Collaboration and Studio Photography](https://post.moma.org/collaboration-and-studio-photography-sanle-sorys-and-ambroise-ngaimokos-portraits/), [MoMA post: Jean Depara](https://post.moma.org/pre-figuring-kinshasa-city-dwellers-in-the-streets-of-leopoldville-the-photography-of-jean-depara), [Wallpaper*: Beauté Congo](https://www.wallpaper.com/art/out-of-africa-paris-fondation-cartier-surveys-100-years-of-modern-art-in-the-dr-congo). *Confidence: medium-high. Black-and-white silver-gelatin prints are the norm for studio portraits of the period (prompts.md), consistent with these collections.*
